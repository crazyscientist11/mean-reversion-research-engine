# Research thesis

## Primary hypothesis

After correcting for deterministic drift and common systematic movements, sufficiently large residual equity dislocations that exhibit stationary behavior, stable mean-reversion parameters, and agreement across independent equilibrium models may display stronger subsequent convergence than raw price deviations.

## Secondary hypothesis

Transaction-cost-aware and risk-aware optimal-stopping thresholds may produce more economically meaningful entry and exit regions than arbitrary fixed z-score rules.

## Null hypotheses

1. Residual filtering does not improve convergence relative to simple price z-scores.
2. OU-derived information does not improve prediction of convergence speed.
3. Optimal-stopping thresholds do not outperform transparent fixed-threshold rules after costs.
4. Model agreement does not improve forward convergence accuracy.

These are hypotheses to test, not established facts.

## Step 2 scope

Step 2 introduces transparent baselines: prior-window log-price z-scores, prior-window linear trend residuals, and prior-window market/sector factor residual returns. Each is evaluated without future observations. These models are comparison tools for later research, not claims about statistical significance, convergence, value, or profitability.
